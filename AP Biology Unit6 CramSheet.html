6.1

DNA and RNA Structure

  • DNA: double-stranded, deoxyribose sugar, uses thymine.
  • RNA: single-stranded, ribose sugar, uses uracil instead of thymine.
  • Central dogma: DNA → (transcription) → RNA → (translation) → Protein.

6.2

DNA Replication

  • Semiconservative: each new DNA molecule = 1 old strand + 1 new strand.
  • Helicase unwinds the double helix; DNA polymerase synthesizes only 5'→3'.
  • Leading strand: continuous. Lagging strand: made in Okazaki fragments, joined by ligase.

6.3

Transcription and RNA Processing

  • RNA polymerase binds the promoter, synthesizes pre-mRNA from the DNA template.
  • Processing: 5' cap + poly-A tail added; introns spliced out, leaving only exons in mature mRNA.

6.4

Translation

  • Ribosome reads mRNA codons (3 nucleotides = 1 amino acid).
  • tRNA anticodon pairs with the codon, delivering the matching amino acid.
  • Starts at AUG; ends at a stop codon → polypeptide released.

6.5

Regulation of Gene Expression

  • Transcription factors bind DNA to help control transcription.
  • Promoter: near the gene, where RNA polymerase binds. Enhancer: can be far away, boosts transcription rate when bound by activators.
  • Repressor: blocks transcription.

6.6

Gene Expression and Cell Specialization

  • All cells share the same DNA; different cell types express different subsets of genes = differential gene expression.
  • Cell differentiation: unspecialized cells become specialized by turning genes on/off.

6.7

Mutations

  • Point mutation (substitution): silent (no aa change), missense (different aa), or nonsense (premature stop).
  • Insertion/deletion (not a multiple of 3) → frameshift, shifting every downstream codon.
Trap: A frameshift is usually far more damaging than a single point mutation — it changes everything downstream.

6.8

Biotechnology

  • Gel electrophoresis: separates DNA by size using an electric current — smaller fragments travel farther.
  • PCR: rapidly amplifies (copies) a specific DNA segment.

⚠

Commonly Missed on the Exam

  • RNA uses uracil, not thymine — double check before writing RNA base pairs.
  • Leading strand = continuous; lagging strand = fragments — know which is which.
  • Promoter vs. enhancer: promoter is close/required; enhancer can be far away and just boosts the rate.
  • Differential gene expression means different genes turned on/off, not different DNA content.
  • Smaller DNA fragments travel farther in gel electrophoresis, not the larger ones.
  • Introns are removed; exons remain in mature mRNA — easy to mix up which is which.
