Free Response
(a) Starting from 1/(1-x)²=Σ(n=1 to ∞)nxⁿ⁻¹ (from differentiating 1/(1-x) once), find a power series for 1/(1-x)³ by differentiating once more.
Sample response: Differentiating again: 2/(1-x)³ = Σ(n=1 to ∞)n(n-1)xⁿ⁻², so 1/(1-x)³ = (1/2)Σn(n-1)xⁿ⁻² (starting from n=2).
(b) Starting from 1/(1+x²)=Σ(-1)ⁿx^(2n), find a power series for arctan(x) using integration.
Sample response: Integrating term-by-term: arctan(x) = Σ(-1)ⁿx^(2n+1)/(2n+1) (the constant is 0, since arctan(0)=0).
(c) Explain why the radius of convergence generally stays the same when a power series is differentiated or integrated term-by-term.
Sample response: Differentiation and integration affect each term's coefficient and power in a way that doesn't change the fundamental growth-rate comparison used by the Ratio Test; the same limiting ratio (involving x) governs convergence before and after these operations, so R is generally preserved, though endpoint behavior can change.
