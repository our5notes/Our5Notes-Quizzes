Historians typically distinguish between long-term underlying causes of World War I, such as militarism, alliance systems, and imperial rivalry, and the immediate triggering event, the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand. Which statement best reflects a sophisticated understanding of the relationship between these different types of causes?

B. Long-term underlying causes created the volatile conditions that allowed the assassination to trigger rapid escalation, meaning both types of causes are necessary to fully explain the outbreak.

Identify ONE long-term underlying cause of World War I.

Explain ONE way that imperial rivalry among European powers contributed to tensions leading up to World War I.

Sample response: Competition among European powers for colonial territory and global influence heightened distrust and rivalry between major states, contributing to an atmosphere of tension that made a large-scale conflict more likely.

Explain ONE way that the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand triggered the outbreak of a broader European war.

Sample response: The assassination gave Austria-Hungary a pretext to declare war on Serbia, and due to the existing alliance system, this regional conflict rapidly escalated as allied nations were drawn into the fighting.
