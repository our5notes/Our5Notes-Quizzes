3.1

Enzyme Structure and Function

Key Points

  • Enzymes are biological catalysts — speed up reactions without being consumed.
  • They work by lowering activation energy.
  • Active site: region where substrate binds.
  • Induced fit model: active site changes shape slightly as it binds the substrate (not a rigid "lock and key").
  • Enzyme returns to its original shape after releasing products — reusable.

3.2

Environmental Impacts on Enzyme Function

Key Points

  • Every enzyme has an optimal temperature & pH; beyond that range, activity drops.
  • Denaturation: extreme heat/pH disrupts weak bonds → enzyme unfolds → loses function.
  • Competitive inhibitor: binds the active site directly, blocking substrate.
  • Noncompetitive inhibitor: binds an allosteric site, changing the enzyme's shape (including the active site).
Trap: More substrate can outcompete a competitive inhibitor, but NOT a noncompetitive one (it doesn't compete for the active site).

3.3

Cellular Energy

Key Points

  • ATP → ADP + Pᵢ: hydrolysis, releases energy = exergonic.
  • ADP + Pᵢ → ATP: phosphorylation, requires energy = endergonic.
  • Energy coupling: pairs ATP hydrolysis (releases energy) with an energy-requiring process, powering it.

3.4

Photosynthesis

Key Points

  • Light reactions (thylakoid): absorb light, split H₂O (releases O₂), produce ATP + NADPH.
  • Calvin cycle (stroma): uses ATP + NADPH to fix CO₂ into organic molecules (G3P → glucose).
  • The two stages are interdependent — Calvin cycle stops without ATP/NADPH from light reactions.

3.5

Cellular Respiration

Key Points

  • Glycolysis (cytoplasm): glucose → 2 pyruvate, small net ATP.
  • Pyruvate oxidation + Krebs cycle (matrix): release CO₂, generate NADH/FADH₂.
  • Electron transport chain (inner membrane): NADH/FADH₂ pass electrons → drives most ATP production; O₂ is the final electron acceptor.
  • No O₂ → ETC stops → ATP production collapses.

⚠

Commonly Missed on the Exam

  • Competitive vs. noncompetitive: only competitive inhibitors are overcome by more substrate.
  • Exergonic = releases energy (ATP → ADP); endergonic = requires energy (ADP → ATP) — easy to flip under pressure.
  • Enzymes are not consumed — don't say they're "used up" in a reaction.
  • The Calvin cycle doesn't need light directly, but stops without ATP/NADPH from the light reactions — don't call it "independent" of light.
  • O₂ is the final electron acceptor in the ETC — without it, the whole chain backs up and stops.
  • Glycolysis happens in the cytoplasm, not the mitochondria — only pyruvate oxidation, the Krebs cycle, and the ETC are mitochondrial.
