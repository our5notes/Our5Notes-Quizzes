Sailors navigating the Indian Ocean trade network relied heavily on predictable seasonal wind patterns known as:

Which type of ship, known for its distinctive triangular sail that allowed for greater maneuverability, was commonly used by Indian Ocean traders, particularly Arab merchants?

B. The dhow, with its distinctive triangular sail, was commonly used by Arab merchants in Indian Ocean trade.

Medium

Port cities along the Indian Ocean trade network, such as Malacca and Calicut, grew wealthy and important primarily because they:

B. Port cities like Malacca and Calicut grew wealthy by serving as key intermediary trading hubs connecting different regions of the Indian Ocean trade network.
4
Mark for Review
Hard

Historians note that Indian Ocean trade, unlike the more centrally coordinated Silk Roads under Mongol control, was instead facilitated by a decentralized network of independent port cities and diverse merchant communities from many different regions and religions. Which factor best explains how this decentralized network was still able to function effectively as an integrated trade system?

B. Shared reliance on monsoon winds, common commercial practices, and the spread of Islam as a unifying trade-friendly religion allowed diverse independent traders to cooperate effectively.

Explain ONE way that monsoon winds shaped the pattern of Indian Ocean trade during this period.

Sample response: Predictable seasonal monsoon winds allowed sailors to plan voyages across the Indian Ocean, sailing in one direction during one season and returning during the opposite monsoon season, shaping the timing and pattern of trade across the network.

Explain ONE way that Islam spread as a result of Indian Ocean trade connections.

Sample response: Muslim merchants trading in Indian Ocean port cities, such as those along the Swahili Coast and in Southeast Asia, spread Islam to local populations through ongoing trade contact and intermarriage, gradually establishing Islamic communities in these port regions.
