A government policy designed to increase birth rates, such as offering financial incentives for having children, is known as:

China's former one-child policy is a well-known historical example of:

B. China's one-child policy was designed to slow population growth, making it a clear example of an anti-natalist policy.

Medium

A country facing a shrinking workforce and aging population might adopt which type of policy to encourage higher birth rates?

B. Countries facing population decline often adopt pro-natalist policies like parental leave and childcare subsidies to encourage higher birth rates.

Hard

A country enacts a pro-natalist policy offering generous parental leave and cash incentives for each additional child, but researchers observe that the country's total fertility rate barely changes over the following decade, since most citizens cite career and housing costs, not financial incentives, as their primary reasons for having fewer children. Which conclusion is best supported by this scenario?

B. This scenario illustrates that population policies may fail to achieve their goals if they do not address the deeper social and economic factors actually driving fertility decisions.

Free Response
Facing a declining population, Country R introduces a pro-natalist policy that includes cash bonuses for each child born and extended paid parental leave.

AExplain the goal of this pro-natalist policy in the context of Country R's demographic situation.

Sample response: The policy aims to raise Country R's birth rate to counteract population decline and address concerns about a shrinking future workforce.

BExplain one potential limitation or obstacle that could prevent this policy from significantly increasing the birth rate.

Sample response: High housing costs or career concerns unrelated to direct financial incentives could continue to discourage larger families regardless of the cash bonuses offered.

CExplain how an anti-natalist policy in a different country facing rapid population growth might differ in its methods from Country R's approach.

Sample response: An anti-natalist policy might instead restrict financial benefits to a limited number of children, promote access to family planning and contraception, or in extreme historical cases impose legal limits on family size.
