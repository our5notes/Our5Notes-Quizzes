4.1

Contextualizing Period 4

  • Election of 1800: first peaceful transfer of power between parties (Federalists → Jefferson's Democratic-Republicans).
  • Marbury v. Madison (1803): established judicial review.
  • Louisiana Purchase (1803) doubled U.S. territory, testing Jefferson's own strict-constructionist views.

4.2

Political Parties & the Era of Jefferson

  • Federalists: strong central government, commercial interests (Hamilton).
  • Democratic-Republicans: limited government, agrarian interests (Jefferson).
  • Louisiana Purchase required Jefferson to set aside his own strict-constructionist views.
Federalist PartyStrict constructionism
The First Party System.
4.3

Politics and Regional Interests

  • Northeast: favors protective tariffs (manufacturing).
  • South: opposes tariffs (plantation economy, imports).
  • West: wants federal internal improvements (roads, canals).
Protective tariffInternal improvements
Competing regional interests.
4.4

America on the World Stage

  • Embargo Act (1807): banned foreign trade, badly hurt the economy.
  • War of 1812: stalemate, but boosted national pride.
  • Monroe Doctrine (1823): declared the Western Hemisphere closed to new European colonization.

4.5

Market Revolution: Industrialization

  • New tech: textile mills, interchangeable parts.
  • Erie Canal (1825) & railroads cut transport costs, connect regional economies into a national market.
  • Deepens North (industry) / South (cotton) specialization.
Interchangeable partsErie Canal
Tech & transportation fuel industry.
4.6

Market Revolution: Society and Culture

  • Wage labor replaces household production.
  • Separate spheres: idealized domestic (women) vs. public (men) roles.
  • Growing class divisions; rapid urbanization.

4.7

Expanding Democracy

  • Property requirements for voting eliminated → universal white male suffrage.
  • More popular, participatory political campaigning.
  • Still excludes women & (in most states) free Black men.

Jackson and Federal Power

  • Indian Removal Act (1830) → Trail of Tears.
  • Nullification Crisis (1832–33): Jackson opposes SC's claim to nullify federal law.
  • Bank War: Jackson vetoes the national bank's recharter.

Development of an American Culture

  • Hudson River School: painters celebrate the American landscape.
  • Transcendentalism (Emerson, Thoreau): individualism, intuition, nature over materialism.

4.10

The Second Great Awakening

  • Religious revival emphasizing personal salvation, spread through camp meetings.
  • Belief that individuals — and society — can be morally perfected.
  • Directly fueled reform movements: abolition, temperance.

⚠

Commonly Missed on the Exam

  • Judicial review comes from Marbury v. Madison (1803), not the Constitution's text directly.
  • The Monroe Doctrine asserted hemispheric influence but the U.S. lacked the military power to fully enforce it at the time.
  • Universal white male suffrage ≠ truly universal suffrage.
  • Jackson's Indian Removal contradicts his image as a champion of democracy — that democracy was for white men only.
  • The Second Great Awakening's belief in moral perfectibility is the direct link to reform movements — know this causal chain.
  • Poor Southern whites supported slavery for racial status, even without owning enslaved people themselves.
