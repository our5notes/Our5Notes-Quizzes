Use the graph of y = f(x), with domain [−4, 4], shown above.

(a) Determine whether f is continuous on the closed interval [1,4]. Justify using the appropriate one-sided limits at each endpoint.
Sample response: Yes. f is a single linear piece on [1,4], with lim(x→1⁺)f(x)=f(1)=2 and lim(x→4⁻)f(x)=f(4)=-1, satisfying one-sided continuity at each endpoint.
(b) Determine whether f is continuous on the full interval [-4,4]. Justify by identifying any point where continuity fails.
Sample response: No. There is a jump discontinuity at x=1, since lim(x→1⁻)f(x)=4 does not equal f(1)=2.
(c) Explain the general difference between checking continuity at an interior point versus at an endpoint of a closed interval.
Sample response: At an interior point, continuity requires the two-sided limit to exist and match the function value. At an endpoint of a closed interval, only the one-sided limit from within the interval's domain is required, since points outside the interval aren't part of what's being examined.
