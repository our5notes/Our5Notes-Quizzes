Bid-rent theory explains how land value and land use intensity typically change as distance from a market or city center:

According to bid-rent theory, farmers located closest to an urban market are most likely to grow which type of product?

B. Farmers closest to the market typically grow perishable, high-value crops that benefit from intensive land use and quick access to consumers.

As distance from the market increases, bid-rent theory predicts that farmers will generally shift toward:

B. As distance from the market increases and land becomes cheaper, farmers shift toward less intensive, more extensive land use.

Two farmers grow the same crop, but Farmer A is located five miles from a major urban market while Farmer B is located 150 miles away. According to bid-rent theory, which farmer is more likely to be paying a higher price per acre for land, and why would this affect the type of farming each is likely to pursue?

B. Farmer A likely pays a higher price for land due to proximity to the market, which incentivizes more intensive, higher-value farming to justify that cost.

Free Response
A region has farms of varying distances from its central urban market: dairy and vegetable farms are clustered close to the city, while cattle ranching and extensive grain operations are located much farther away.

AExplain how bid-rent theory accounts for the location of the dairy and vegetable farms close to the city.

Sample response: Dairy and vegetable products are perishable and high-value, so farmers are willing to pay the higher land prices near the city in exchange for quick access to market and reduced spoilage.

BExplain how bid-rent theory accounts for the location of the cattle ranching and grain operations farther from the city.

Sample response: Cattle ranching and grain farming require large amounts of relatively cheap land and are less perishable or more easily transported, making the lower land prices farther from the city more economically practical for these extensive land uses.

CExplain what would likely happen to land use patterns near the city if transportation costs to bring goods to market dropped significantly.

Sample response: If transportation costs dropped significantly, farmers producing less perishable goods farther from the city could compete more easily, potentially allowing extensive land uses to persist closer to the city or shifting the overall pattern of rings predicted by bid-rent theory.
