Which concept best explains this pattern, and how does it differ from a single point of agricultural origin followed by diffusion?

Archaeological evidence shows that agriculture developed independently in Mesopotamia, Mesoamerica, and parts of East Asia at roughly similar times, with no evidence of direct contact between these early farming societies.

AExplain what this evidence suggests about the origins of agriculture (independent invention versus diffusion from a single hearth).

Sample response: This evidence suggests agriculture arose through independent invention in multiple separate hearths, rather than diffusing outward from a single original point of origin.

BIdentify one specific crop or agricultural practice associated with one of these agricultural hearths.

Sample response: Maize (corn) cultivation is closely associated with the Mesoamerican agricultural hearth.

CExplain how cultural diffusion could have later spread agricultural practices from one of these hearths to a neighboring region.

Sample response: Once established in a hearth, agricultural knowledge and crops could spread to neighboring regions through trade, migration, or contact between different groups, a process of cultural diffusion distinct from the original independent invention.
