For a semicircular cross-section with diameter equal to the base region's height, the radius is:

For a semicircular cross-section using diameter=√x (radius=√x/2), a student mistakenly uses radius=√x instead. What effect does this error have on the computed volume?

B. Using the wrong (doubled) radius quadruples the cross-sectional area at every point, drastically overestimating the volume.

Free Response

The base region is bounded by y=√x, y=0, and x=4 (the same region from Topic 8.7).

(a) Cross-sections perpendicular to the x-axis are equilateral triangles with side length √x. Set up and evaluate the volume.
Sample response: A(x)=(√3/4)(√x)²=(√3/4)x. V=(√3/4)∫(0 to 4)x dx = (√3/4)(8) = 2√3 ≈ 3.46 cubic units.
(b) Cross-sections are instead semicircles with diameter √x. Set up and evaluate the volume.
Sample response: r=√x/2. A(x)=(1/2)π(x/4)=πx/8. V=(π/8)∫(0 to 4)x dx = (π/8)(8) = π ≈ 3.14 cubic units.
(c) Compare your answers to (a) and (b) with the square cross-section volume (8) from Topic 8.7. Which shape gives the smallest volume, and does this make sense given the area formulas?
Sample response: The semicircle gives the smallest volume (≈3.14), followed by the triangle (≈3.46), with the square giving the largest (8). This makes sense since, for the same characteristic length √x, the square's area formula (side²) produces the largest cross-sectional area of the three shapes.
