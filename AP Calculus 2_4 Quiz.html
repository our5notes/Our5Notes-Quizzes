Free Response

Consider the four graph types discussed in class, each showing a different reason a derivative fails to exist at x=1.

(a) For a function with a CORNER at x=1, describe what happens to the slope approaching from the left versus the right.
Sample response: The slope approaching from the left is a different finite value than the slope approaching from the right, so the one-sided derivatives disagree, meaning the two-sided derivative doesn't exist.
(b) For a function with a JUMP DISCONTINUITY at x=1, explain why the derivative cannot exist there, referencing continuity as a prerequisite.
Sample response: Since differentiability requires continuity as a necessary first condition, and a jump discontinuity means the function isn't even continuous at that point, the derivative cannot exist there — there's no need to even examine slopes.
(c) True or False, with justification: "If f is differentiable at x=a, then f must be continuous at x=a, and if f is continuous at x=a, then f must be differentiable at x=a."
Sample response: Only half true. Differentiability does imply continuity (TRUE). However, continuity does NOT imply differentiability (FALSE) — a function can be continuous at a point yet fail to be differentiable there due to a corner, cusp, or vertical tangent.
