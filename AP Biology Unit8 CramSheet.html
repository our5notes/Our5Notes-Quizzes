8.1

Responses to the Environment

  • Endotherms: generate/regulate own body heat → stable temp, but costly (more food needed).
  • Ectotherms: rely on external heat → body temp tracks environment, but cheaper energetically.
  • Behavioral & physiological responses are shaped by natural selection when they improve fitness.

8.2

Energy Flow Through Ecosystems

  • Energy flows: Producers → Primary consumers → Secondary consumers → Tertiary consumers.
  • Only about 10% of energy transfers to the next trophic level; the rest is lost as heat/used for metabolism.
  • This is why ecosystems support far fewer top predators than producers.

8.3

Population Ecology

  • Exponential growth: J-shaped curve, constant rate, unlimited resources (unsustainable long-term).
  • Logistic growth: S-shaped curve, growth slows as population approaches carrying capacity (K).

8.4

Effect of Density on Populations

  • Density-dependent: effect grows stronger as population gets more crowded (disease, competition, predation).
  • Density-independent: affects a population regardless of density (fires, storms, natural disasters).
Trap: A wildfire is density-independent even though it can drastically reduce population size — its impact doesn't scale with crowding.

8.5

Community Ecology

  • Predation (+/−), Competition (−/−), Mutualism (+/+), Commensalism (+/0), Parasitism (+/−).
  • Competing for the same limited resource → the more efficient species can competitively exclude the other.

8.6

Biodiversity

  • Species richness (# of species) + species evenness (how evenly distributed) = biodiversity.
  • Keystone species: disproportionately large effect on community relative to abundance — removal triggers a trophic cascade (e.g., sea otters → urchins → kelp).

8.7

Disruptions to Ecosystems

  • Invasive species: outcompete natives, disrupt community interactions.
  • Biomagnification: toxin concentration increases at each successive trophic level — worst in top predators.
  • Eutrophication: excess nutrients → algal bloom → decomposition depletes O₂ → fish kills.

⚠

Commonly Missed on the Exam

  • ~10% energy transfer between trophic levels — the reason ecosystems support far fewer top predators than producers.
  • Exponential growth is never sustainable long-term — always limited eventually by resources.
  • Density-independent factors can still cause major population declines — "independent" refers to the mechanism, not the severity.
  • Biomagnification increases toward top predators — don't reverse the direction.
  • A keystone species isn't necessarily abundant — its impact is disproportionate to its numbers.
  • Commensalism is +/0, not +/+ — the second species must be genuinely unaffected, not just "a little helped."
