Question 1 Easy

What term describes the loss of an enzyme's shape and function due to extreme heat?

AHydrolysis process
BDenaturation process
CPhosphorylation process
DReplication process
Denaturation is the loss of a protein's shape and function due to extreme conditions.
Question 2 Medium

Using the diagram shown, a competitive inhibitor blocks an enzyme by binding directly to which location?

AThe allosteric site directly
BThe active site directly
CThe carboxyl terminus directly
DThe signal sequence directly
Competitive inhibitors bind directly to the active site, blocking the substrate.
Question 3 Medium

Using the diagram shown, a noncompetitive inhibitor changes an enzyme's shape by binding to which location?

AThe active site directly
BAn allosteric site instead
CThe substrate molecule directly
DThe reaction product directly
Noncompetitive inhibitors bind an allosteric site, changing the enzyme's overall shape.
Question 4 Hard

Increasing substrate concentration can often overcome a competitive inhibitor's effect. Why does this strategy NOT work for a noncompetitive inhibitor?

ANoncompetitive inhibitors bind the active site more tightly
BNoncompetitive inhibitors change enzyme shape regardless of substrate
CNoncompetitive inhibitors are permanently bonded and irremovable
DNoncompetitive inhibitors only act at very high temperatures
Because noncompetitive inhibitors bind elsewhere and alter enzyme shape, more substrate cannot compete them off.
Free Response FRQ

Refer to the diagrams shown above.

(a) Identify where a competitive inhibitor binds on an enzyme, based on the diagram shown.
Sample response: A competitive inhibitor binds directly to the enzyme's active site, as shown in the diagram.
(b) Explain why a noncompetitive inhibitor can reduce enzyme activity without binding the active site at all.
Sample response: A noncompetitive inhibitor binds to an allosteric site, a location separate from the active site, and this binding changes the overall shape of the enzyme, including the active site, reducing the enzyme's ability to bind its substrate even though the inhibitor never touches the active site directly.
(c) Explain, at the molecular level, why an enzyme exposed to extremely high temperatures typically loses its function.
Sample response: Extremely high temperatures disrupt the hydrogen bonds and other weak interactions holding the enzyme's secondary and tertiary structure together, causing the protein to unfold and lose its specific three-dimensional shape; since the enzyme's function depends on this precise shape, losing it destroys the enzyme's ability to bind its substrate.
