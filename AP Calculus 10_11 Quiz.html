Use the graph of cos(x) with its Taylor polynomial approximations shown above.

(a) Verify that P₂(x)=1-x²/2 by computing the necessary derivatives of cos(x) at x=0.
Sample response: f(0)=1. f'(x)=-sin(x)→f'(0)=0. f''(x)=-cos(x)→f''(0)=-1. P₂(x)=1+0·x+(-1/2)x²=1-x²/2.
(b) Extend to find P₄(x), the 4th-degree Maclaurin polynomial for cos(x).
Sample response: f'''(0)=0, f⁗(0)=1. P₄(x) = 1-x²/2+x⁴/24.
(c) Use P₂(x) to approximate cos(0.2), and compare to the actual value cos(0.2)≈0.9801.
Sample response: P₂(0.2)=1-0.04/2=1-0.02=0.98. This is very close to the actual value 0.9801.
