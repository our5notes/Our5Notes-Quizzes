Russia's system of government is best described as a:

B. Russia's system of government is best described as a semi-presidential (dual executive) system.
2
Mark for Review
Medium

The Russian president holds which of the following powers not held by the prime minister?

B. The Russian president holds the power to issue decrees that carry the force of law without legislative approval.
3
Mark for Review
Medium

Russia's Federation Council, the upper house of its legislature, is composed of:

B. The Federation Council is composed of two members appointed from each region, one by the regional governor and one by the regional legislature.
4
Mark for Review
Hard

Historians note that although Russia's Constitutional Court technically possesses the power of judicial review, in practice it rarely exercises this authority to challenge the president. Which statement best explains the broader significance of this pattern?

B. This pattern reflects the broader concentration of real political power in the presidency rather than in independent judicial institutions.

Short Answer Practice

5
Mark for Review
Short Answer

Explain why Russia's system is described as a "dual executive," and identify one power held by the president that is not held by the prime minister.

Sample response: It is called a dual executive because power is split between a president (head of state) and a prime minister (head of government). The president alone holds powers such as issuing decrees with the force of law and dissolving the Duma, powers the prime minister does not hold.
6
Mark for Review
Short Answer

Compare the composition and powers of the Duma and the Federation Council.

Sample response: The Duma is the elected lower house with 450 deputies who pass bills, approve budgets, and confirm the prime minister; the Federation Council is the appointed upper house, with two members from each region, that approves budgets, treaties, judicial nominees, and troop deployments, but has comparatively limited independent political power.
7
Mark for Review
Short Answer

Explain the relationship between the Russian government and the oligarchs, using Mikhail Khodorkovsky's experience as an example.

Sample response: Oligarchs like those who supported Yeltsin generally maintain their wealth and influence by remaining loyal to the state; Khodorkovsky, by contrast, publicly criticized Putin and was subsequently forced into bankruptcy, illustrating that oligarch wealth in Russia is closely tied to political loyalty rather than independent from state power.
