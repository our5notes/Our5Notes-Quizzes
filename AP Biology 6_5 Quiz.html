Topic 6.5 Quiz: Regulation of Gene Expression

Figure for Questions Below
Activators bound to enhancers switch a gene ON; repressors bound near the promoter switch a gene OFF.
Question 1 Easy

What term describes a protein that binds DNA to help control whether a gene is transcribed?

AA ribosome protein
BA transcription factor
CA tRNA molecule
DA codon sequence
A transcription factor is a protein that binds DNA to help control gene transcription.
Question 2 Medium

Using the diagram shown, what happens to transcription when a repressor binds near the promoter?

ATranscription increases significantly overall
BTranscription is blocked or reduced
CTranscription rate stays exactly the same
DTranscription begins at a new site
A repressor binding near the promoter blocks or reduces transcription.
Question 3 Medium

What is the name of the DNA sequence where RNA polymerase binds to begin transcription?

AThe enhancer sequence
BThe promoter sequence
CThe intron sequence
DThe operator sequence
The promoter is where RNA polymerase binds to begin transcription.
Question 4 Hard

Using the diagram shown, a mutation disrupts a gene's promoter region, but the gene's coding sequence remains completely normal. What is the most likely outcome?

AThe gene would be transcribed at a higher rate than usual
BTranscription could be reduced or prevented despite the normal gene
CThe resulting protein would fold into a different final shape
DThe mutation would have no effect on the gene at all
A disrupted promoter can prevent or reduce transcription, even if the gene's coding sequence is unaffected.
Free Response FRQ

Refer to the diagrams shown above.

(a) Identify what happens to gene expression when an activator is bound, based on the diagram shown.
Sample response: When an activator is bound, as shown in the diagram, gene expression (transcription) proceeds and the gene is turned on.
(b) Explain the difference between a promoter and an enhancer.
Sample response: A promoter is located near the gene it controls and is where RNA polymerase binds to initiate transcription; an enhancer can be located far from the gene and increases the rate of transcription when bound by activator proteins.
(c) Explain how a repressor can block transcription even without altering the gene's own coding sequence.
Sample response: A repressor binds to a regulatory DNA sequence near the gene and physically blocks RNA polymerase from properly initiating transcription, silencing the gene's expression without changing the sequence of the gene itself.
