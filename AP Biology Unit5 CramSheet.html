5.1

Meiosis

Key Points

  • Produces 4 haploid gametes from 1 diploid parent cell.
  • Meiosis I: homologous chromosomes separate (diploid → haploid).
  • Meiosis II: sister chromatids separate (like mitosis) — chromosome number stays the same.
  • DNA replicates once (S phase), before meiosis begins.
HaploidDiploidHomologous chromosomes
Trap: Meiosis I separates homologs; Meiosis II separates sister chromatids — the #1 most confused fact in this topic.

5.2

Meiosis and Genetic Diversity

Key Points

  • Crossing over (Prophase I): homologous chromosomes exchange DNA segments → recombinant chromosomes.
  • Independent assortment (Metaphase I): random orientation of homolog pairs → 2²³ possible combinations in humans.
  • Random fertilization multiplies diversity even further.

5.3

Mendelian Genetics

Key Points

  • Law of segregation: the 2 alleles for a gene separate into different gametes.
  • Law of independent assortment: alleles for different genes (different chromosomes) assort independently.
  • Homozygous = 2 identical alleles. Heterozygous = 2 different alleles.
  • Punnett square: predicts genotype/phenotype ratios of offspring.

5.4

Non-Mendelian Genetics

Key Points

  • Incomplete dominance: heterozygote shows a blended phenotype (red × white → pink).
  • Codominance: heterozygote shows both phenotypes fully & separately (AB blood type).
  • Linked genes: same chromosome, inherited together unless separated by crossing over.
  • Sex-linked genes: located on the X or Y chromosome.
Trap: Incomplete dominance = a BLEND; codominance = BOTH shown fully — don't mix these up.

5.5

Environmental Effects on Phenotype

Key Points

  • Phenotype = genotype + environment — genotype alone doesn't fully determine phenotype.
  • Classic example: Himalayan rabbit — a temperature-sensitive enzyme produces dark pigment only in cooler extremities (ears, nose, paws).
  • Norm of reaction: range of phenotypes one genotype can produce across environments.

⚠

Commonly Missed on the Exam

  • Meiosis I vs. II: homologs separate in I, sister chromatids separate in II.
  • Crossing over happens in Prophase I; independent assortment happens in Metaphase I — different mechanisms, different timing.
  • Incomplete dominance (blend) vs. codominance (both shown) — always check whether the trait blends or stays separate.
  • Environmental effects on phenotype don't change the genotype — only which genes get expressed.
