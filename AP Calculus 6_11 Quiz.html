For ∫1/(x²-6x+13)dx, a student is unsure whether to try factoring the denominator or completing the square. Given that the discriminant of x²-6x+13 is negative (36-52=-16), which technique is appropriate, and why?

B. A negative discriminant confirms the quadratic has no real roots, so it cannot be factored — completing the square is the correct approach.

For each integral, state which technique you'll use, then evaluate.

(a) ∫x²(x³-2)⁴dx
Sample response: U-substitution: u=x³-2, du=3x²dx. (1/3)∫u⁴du = (1/3)(u⁵/5) = (x³-2)⁵/15 + C.
(b) ∫(2x³-x+3)/x dx
Sample response: Split the fraction: 2x²-1+3/x. Integrating: (2/3)x³-x+3ln|x|+C.
(c) ∫1/(x²-2x+5)dx
Sample response: Complete the square: x²-2x+5=(x-1)²+4. Integral: (1/2)arctan((x-1)/2)+C.
