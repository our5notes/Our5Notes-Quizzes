Cell signaling proceeds through three stages: reception, transduction, and response.
Question 1 Easy

What is the first stage of cell signaling, in which a ligand binds a receptor?

ATransduction stage
BReception stage
CResponse stage

Reception is the first stage, when a ligand binds a specific receptor.

AA ligand binds directly to a receptor protein
BRelay molecules pass along and amplify the signal
CThe cell carries out its final specific response

During transduction, relay molecules pass along and amplify the signal inside the cell.

AReception stage
BTransduction stage
CResponse stage

Question 4 Hard

A cell has a normal receptor but a mutation disables a relay protein in its transduction pathway. What would most likely happen when the ligand binds?

AThe cell would respond normally despite the mutation
BThe signal would fail to reach the response stage properly
CThe receptor would bind the ligand more tightly
DThe cell would produce an entirely new receptor type
A broken relay protein disrupts transduction, preventing the signal from reaching the response stage.
Free Response FRQ

Refer to the diagram shown above.

(a) Identify the three stages of cell signaling shown in the diagram, in order.
Sample response: The three stages shown are reception, transduction, and response.
(b) Explain what is meant by signal amplification during the transduction stage.
Sample response: Signal amplification refers to how a single signaling event can trigger a cascade of many downstream molecules being activated in sequence, so the effect of the original signal is greatly magnified by the time it reaches the response stage.
(c) Predict what would happen to a cell's response if its receptor protein were completely absent, and explain your reasoning.
Sample response: Without a receptor protein, the cell would be unable to bind the signaling molecule at all, so the reception stage could never begin, meaning the transduction and response stages would also never occur, regardless of how much signal is present.
