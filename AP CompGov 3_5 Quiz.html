Chechnya, a majority-Muslim region of Russia, has been a persistent source of conflict primarily due to:

B. Chechnya has been a persistent source of conflict due to a strong independence movement, valuable resources, and a history of conflict including terrorist attacks.

Hard

Historians note that while Russia allows some civil society activity in areas like academia, sports, and charity work, the government retains and has used the power to shut down organizations it views as threatening to the state. Which statement best explains the significance of this pattern for understanding Russian civil society?

B. Civil society in Russia exists only within limits the government is willing to tolerate, since the state retains and has exercised the power to shut down organizations it perceives as threatening.

Short Answer

Explain how economic liberalization has been applied in Russia, using at least one specific policy example.

Sample response: Economic liberalization in Russia has included privatizing formerly state-owned industries and reducing government regulations, taxes, and subsidies, shifting more economic activity toward free-market mechanisms rather than central planning.

Explain why Chechnya has remained a persistent challenge for the Russian government.

Sample response: Chechnya has remained a persistent challenge because it is a majority-Muslim region with valuable resources like oil, a strong independence movement, and a history of conflict with the Russian government, including terrorist attacks linked to the broader struggle for Chechen independence.

Explain why civil society in Russia is often described as limited or conditional, using the government's power to shut down organizations as evidence.

Sample response: While Russia allows some civil society activity in areas like academia, sports, and charity work, the government retains and has used the power to shut down organizations it views as threatening to the state, meaning civil society exists only within limits the government is willing to tolerate.
