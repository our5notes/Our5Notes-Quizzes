Free Response FRQ

Refer to the diagram shown above.

(a) Using the diagram shown, identify the act that triggered “Bleeding Kansas.”
Sample response: The Kansas-Nebraska Act, shown in the diagram, triggered “Bleeding Kansas.”
(b) Explain why the Kansas-Nebraska Act proved so destabilizing to sectional peace.
Sample response: By applying popular sovereignty to Kansas and Nebraska, the Kansas-Nebraska Act effectively repealed the Missouri Compromise's long-standing line dividing free and slave territory, reopening a question that many had believed settled; this led pro- and anti-slavery settlers to rush into Kansas to influence the vote, producing violent conflict (“Bleeding Kansas”) that previewed the larger sectional violence to come.
(c) Explain why the Dred Scott decision alarmed many Northerners, even those who were not strongly abolitionist.
Sample response: The Dred Scott decision went beyond simply ruling on one enslaved man's case; the Court also declared that Congress had no constitutional authority to ban slavery in any federal territory at all, effectively striking down the entire basis of the free-soil political position; this alarmed many Northerners who feared the decision meant slavery could potentially expand anywhere, even into territories or states that had previously banned it.
