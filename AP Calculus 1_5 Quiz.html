If lim(x→a)f(x)=5 and lim(x→a)g(x)=3, then lim(x→a)[f(x)+g(x)] equals:

B. By the sum law, the limit of a sum equals the sum of the limits: 5+3=8.

If lim(x→a)f(x)=4 and lim(x→a)g(x)=-2, then lim(x→a)[f(x)·g(x)] equals:

B. By the product law, 4×(-2)=-8.

If lim(x→a)f(x)=6 and lim(x→a)g(x)=0, which limit law CANNOT be directly applied to find lim(x→a)[f(x)/g(x)]?

C. The quotient law requires the denominator's limit to be nonzero, which fails here.

Suppose lim(x→2)f(x)=0 and lim(x→2)g(x)=0. Which statement is true about lim(x→2)[f(x)/g(x)]?

C. 0/0 is an indeterminate form; the quotient law doesn't apply, and further work (factoring, etc.) is needed.

Suppose lim(x→3)f(x)=8 and lim(x→3)g(x)=-4.

(a) Find lim(x→3)[3f(x)-2g(x)], showing which limit laws you use.
Sample response: Using the constant multiple and difference laws: 3(8)-2(-4) = 24+8 = 32.
(b) Find lim(x→3)[f(x)]².
Sample response: By the power law (a special case of the product law): 8² = 64.
(c) Determine both lim(x→3)[g(x)/f(x)] and lim(x→3)[f(x)/g(x)], explaining why the quotient law applies to both.
Sample response: Since both denominators (f→8 and g→-4) are nonzero, the quotient law applies to both: g/f → -4/8 = -0.5, and f/g → 8/-4 = -2.
