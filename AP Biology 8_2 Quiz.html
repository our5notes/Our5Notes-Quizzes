Question 1 Easy

What percentage of energy is typically transferred from one trophic level to the next?

AAbout 1 percent
BAbout 10 percent
CAbout 50 percent
DAbout 90 percent
Only about 10% of energy is typically transferred between successive trophic levels.

AThe producers level

Producers, at the base of the pyramid, contain the greatest amount of available energy.
Question 3 Medium

Producers capture energy from sunlight through photosynthesis.
Question 4 Hard

Using the pyramid shown, explain why an ecosystem can typically support far fewer top predators than producers.

AOnly about 10% of energy transfers at each successive level
BTop predators require much larger territories than producers
CProducers reproduce far more slowly than top predators do
DTop predators consume ten times more energy than needed
Since only about 10% of energy transfers at each level, far less energy remains available for top predators.
Free Response FRQ

Refer to the energy pyramid shown above.

(a) Identify which trophic level shown has the least available energy.
Sample response: The tertiary consumer level, shown at the top of the pyramid, has the least available energy.
(b) Explain why energy transfer between trophic levels is described as inefficient.
Sample response: At each transfer between trophic levels, only about 10% of the energy is passed on, with the rest lost as heat or used for the organism's own metabolic processes, making the transfer inefficient.
(c) Explain why eating lower on the food chain is generally a more energy-efficient way for humans to obtain calories.
Sample response: Since only about 10% of energy transfers at each level, eating an animal that itself ate plants means humans receive only about 10% of the energy originally captured by the plants; eating plants directly captures much more of the original energy.
