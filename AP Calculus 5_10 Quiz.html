(a) Find two positive numbers whose sum is 30 and whose product is maximized. Show the function, the critical point, and confirm it gives a maximum.
Sample response: Let x+y=30, y=30-x. P(x)=x(30-x)=30x-x². P'(x)=30-2x=0 → x=15. P''(x)=-2<0 → confirms a maximum. y=15. The numbers are 15 and 15, giving a maximum product of 225.
(b) A rectangular garden is to have an area of 200 square feet. Express the perimeter P as a function of one side length x, and find the dimensions that minimize the perimeter.
Sample response: xy=200 → y=200/x. P(x)=2x+400/x. P'(x)=2-400/x²=0 → x²=200 → x=10√2≈14.14 ft. P''(x)=800/x³>0 → confirms a minimum. y=10√2 as well — the garden is a square, approximately 14.14 ft by 14.14 ft.
(c) Explain why checking that a critical point actually gives a MAXIMUM (not a minimum) is an essential final step in an optimization problem.
Sample response: Setting the derivative to zero only finds critical points — candidates for extrema — but doesn't by itself confirm whether that point is a maximum, minimum, or neither; without confirming via the Second Derivative Test, First Derivative Test, or Candidates Test, you might mistakenly report the wrong type of extremum as your final answer.
