Question 1 Easy

As a cell increases in size, what happens to its surface-area-to-volume ratio?

AIt increases steadily
BIt decreases steadily
CIt stays exactly constant
DIt fluctuates randomly
Volume grows faster than surface area as size increases, so the SA:V ratio decreases.

Question 4 Hard

A cell with very high metabolic demands would most likely benefit from which characteristic, based on surface-area-to-volume principles?

AA larger overall size to maximize internal storage
BA smaller size or shape with greater surface area
CA thicker plasma membrane to slow down diffusion
DFewer mitochondria to reduce its energy demands

Free Response FRQ

Refer to the cube diagrams shown above.

(a) Calculate the surface-area-to-volume ratio for a cube with sides of 4 units.
Sample response: SA = 6(4)² = 96. V = 4³ = 64. SA:V = 96:64 = 1.5:1.
(b) Explain why the surface-area-to-volume ratio decreases as a cube's side length increases.
Sample response: Surface area scales with the square of side length while volume scales with the cube of side length, so as side length increases, volume grows faster than surface area, causing the ratio between them to decrease.
(c) Explain the biological consequence of a low surface-area-to-volume ratio for a very large cell.
Sample response: A very large cell with a low surface-area-to-volume ratio has relatively little membrane surface available to exchange nutrients, gases, and wastes for its large internal volume, making it difficult to meet the cell's metabolic needs efficiently, which is one major reason cells tend to remain small.
