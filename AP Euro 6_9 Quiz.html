Historians note that governments responded to the social effects of industrialization through a range of institutional reforms, including investment in public health infrastructure such as sewage systems, large-scale urban redesign projects like Haussmann's renovation of Paris, and expanded access to public education. Which statement best explains the broader historical significance of these institutional responses?

B. Institutional responses reflect direct government efforts to manage and mitigate the social consequences that rapid industrialization and urbanization had produced.

5
Mark for Review
Short Answer

Identify ONE public health challenge created by rapid urbanization during industrialization.

Sample response: Rapid urbanization during industrialization contributed to the spread of infectious diseases such as cholera, driven by inadequate sanitation and unsafe drinking water in overcrowded neighborhoods.
6
Mark for Review
Short Answer

Explain ONE way that large-scale urban redesign projects, such as Haussmann's renovation of Paris, aimed to address problems caused by industrialization and urban growth.

Sample response: Haussmann's renovation of Paris aimed to improve sanitation, circulation, and public order by redesigning city streets, housing, and public spaces in response to the overcrowding and disorganized growth associated with rapid industrialization.
7
Mark for Review
Short Answer

Explain ONE reason why governments expanded access to public education during the nineteenth century.

Sample response: Governments expanded access to public education to increase literacy and prepare workers with the skills needed for an increasingly industrial economy, while also using education in some cases to foster national identity among an increasingly urbanized population.
