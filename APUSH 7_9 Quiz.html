Free Response FRQ

Refer to the diagram shown above.

(a) Using the diagram shown, identify the event in October 1929 that triggered the Great Depression.
Sample response: The stock market crash, shown in the diagram, triggered the Great Depression in October 1929.
(b) Explain how underlying economic weaknesses of the 1920s made the Great Depression more severe once the crash occurred.
Sample response: Even before the crash, the 1920s economy suffered from overproduction (factories producing more than consumers could afford to buy), risky and often unregulated stock market speculation (including buying stock “on margin” with borrowed money), and highly uneven wealth distribution that limited how much ordinary consumers could spend; these underlying weaknesses meant the economy had little cushion to absorb the shock of the stock market crash, allowing a financial crisis to spiral into a prolonged, severe economic depression.
(c) Explain the chain reaction by which the stock market crash led to widespread unemployment.
Sample response: The stock market crash wiped out enormous amounts of wealth and confidence, causing panicked bank depositors to withdraw their savings, which led to widespread bank failures; as banks failed and credit became scarce, businesses could not obtain loans to operate or expand, forcing many to cut production and lay off workers, while reduced consumer spending (as people lost savings and jobs) further reduced business revenue, creating a self-reinforcing downward economic spiral.
