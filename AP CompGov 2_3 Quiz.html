Historians note that under Great Britain's First Past the Post electoral system, a party's share of parliamentary seats often does not closely match its share of the national popular vote, a pattern especially pronounced for smaller parties like the Liberal Democrats. Which statement best explains the broader significance of this mismatch?

Short Answer

Explain ONE reason why the Liberal Democrats have historically been underrepresented in parliamentary seats relative to their share of the popular vote.

Sample response: Because First Past the Post rewards concentrated support within individual districts, the Liberal Democrats' support, which has historically been spread relatively evenly across the country, has translated into far fewer seats than their national vote share would suggest.

Short Answer

Explain ONE general trend in UK general election results between 2010 and 2024.

Sample response: The Conservative Party won consistently from 2010 through 2019, but the 2024 election marked a sharp reversal, with a landslide win for the Labour Party and steep declines for both the Conservatives and the SNP.
