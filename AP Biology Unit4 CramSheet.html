4.1

Cell Communication

Key Points

  • Direct contact: signals pass through gap junctions/plasmodesmata between adjacent cells.
  • Paracrine (local): signal diffuses through extracellular fluid to nearby cells.
  • Endocrine (long-distance): hormone travels through the bloodstream to distant target cells.
  • Only cells with the matching receptor can respond to a given signal.

4.2

Introduction to Signal Transduction

Key Points

  • Reception: ligand binds a specific receptor.
  • Transduction: relay molecules pass along & amplify the signal inside the cell.
  • Response: the cell carries out a specific change (enzyme activation, gene expression, etc.).

4.3

Signal Transduction Pathways

Key Points

  • GPCR pathway: ligand binds GPCR → activates G protein → activates enzyme (adenylyl cyclase) → produces second messenger (cAMP) → triggers response.
  • Phosphorylation cascade: kinases activate other kinases by adding phosphate groups — amplifies the signal at each step.

4.4

Feedback

Key Points

  • Negative feedback: response REVERSES the stimulus → maintains homeostasis (most common; e.g., insulin lowering blood glucose).
  • Positive feedback: response AMPLIFIES the stimulus → drives a process to completion (rare; e.g., childbirth, blood clotting).
Trap: Most physiological regulation is negative feedback — positive feedback is the exception, used only when a process needs to finish quickly.

4.5

Cell Cycle

Key Points

  • Interphase (G1, S, G2): cell grows & replicates DNA — takes up most of the cycle.
  • S phase: DNA replication occurs here.
  • M phase (mitosis): chromosomes separate into two nuclei.
  • Cytokinesis: cytoplasm physically divides into two daughter cells.

4.6

Regulation of Cell Cycle

Key Points

  • Checkpoints (G1, G2, M/spindle) monitor the cell and can halt the cycle if problems (like DNA damage) are detected.
  • Cyclins + Cdks (cyclin-dependent kinases) drive the cell through checkpoints.
  • Disabled checkpoints → damaged cells divide unchecked → contributes to cancer.

⚠

Commonly Missed on the Exam

  • GPCR order: ligand → receptor → G protein → enzyme → second messenger → response. Know this chain exactly.
  • A cell needs the receptor, not just exposure to the ligand, to respond — memorize this for "why doesn't X cell respond" questions.
  • Signal amplification happens during transduction, not reception or response.
  • Negative feedback ≠ "bad" — it's the normal, stabilizing mechanism; don't assume "positive" is better.
  • Cells spend most of their time in interphase, not mitosis.
  • A disabled checkpoint (not a disabled cyclin alone) is the most direct link to uncontrolled division/cancer.
