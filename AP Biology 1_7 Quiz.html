Free Response FRQ
(a) Describe what determines an amino acid's unique chemical properties.
Sample response: An amino acid's chemical properties are determined by its variable R group (side chain), which differs among the 20 amino acids and can be nonpolar, polar, acidic, or basic.
(b) Explain the difference between a protein's secondary structure and its tertiary structure.
Sample response: Secondary structure refers to local folding patterns, like alpha helices and beta sheets, formed by hydrogen bonds along the polypeptide backbone, while tertiary structure is the overall three-dimensional shape of the entire polypeptide, formed by interactions among the R groups.
(c) Explain why a protein that has been denatured typically loses its biological function, even though its primary structure remains unchanged.
Sample response: Denaturation disrupts the bonds and interactions that hold a protein's secondary, tertiary, and quaternary structure together, causing it to unfold and lose its specific three-dimensional shape; since a protein's function depends on this precise shape, losing that shape destroys the protein's function even though its underlying amino acid sequence hasn't changed.
