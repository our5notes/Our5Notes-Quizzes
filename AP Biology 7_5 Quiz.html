The Hardy-Weinberg equation predicts genotype frequencies (p² + 2pq + q² = 1) in a non-evolving population.
Question 1 Easy

In the Hardy-Weinberg equation, what does the term 2pq represent?

AHomozygous dominant frequency
BHeterozygous frequency value
CHomozygous recessive frequency
DTotal population size value
2pq represents the frequency of heterozygous individuals in the population.

A0.16 frequency value

Question 3 Medium

Which of the following is one of the five conditions required for Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium?

AHigh mutation rate present
BRandom mating occurring
CSmall population size present
DStrong natural selection occurring
Random mating is one of the five required conditions for Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium.
Question 4 Hard

Using the diagram shown, explain why real populations are rarely in perfect Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium.

AReal populations always have exactly equal allele frequencies
BThe five required idealized conditions are essentially never met
CHardy-Weinberg equilibrium only applies to extinct populations
DMutation rates are always zero in every real population
The five idealized conditions required for equilibrium are essentially never fully met in reality.
Free Response FRQ

Refer to the diagram shown above.

(a) Using the diagram shown, state the genotype frequency represented by q².
Sample response: The genotype frequency represented by q², shown in the diagram, is the homozygous recessive frequency.
(b) A population has 16% recessive phenotype individuals. Calculate p and q, assuming Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium.
Sample response: q² = 0.16, so q = √0.16 = 0.4. Since p+q=1, p = 1−0.4 = 0.6.
(c) Explain why real populations are rarely in perfect Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium.
Sample response: Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium requires five idealized conditions, such as no mutation, random mating, and no gene flow, that are essentially never fully met in real populations, so real populations are almost always evolving to some degree rather than remaining in perfect equilibrium.
