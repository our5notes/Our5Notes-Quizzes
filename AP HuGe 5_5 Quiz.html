Easy

The Green Revolution, which spread high-yield crop varieties, synthetic fertilizers, and irrigation technology to developing countries in the mid-to-late twentieth century, primarily aimed to:

B. The Green Revolution aimed to increase agricultural yields and reduce hunger in developing regions through new technologies.
2
Mark for Review
Medium

Which of the following is a commonly cited environmental drawback of Green Revolution farming practices?

B. Green Revolution practices' heavy reliance on chemical fertilizers and pesticides is commonly cited as an environmental drawback, contributing to water pollution and soil degradation.
3
Mark for Review
Medium

Critics have noted that Green Revolution technologies, such as expensive high-yield seeds and irrigation equipment, have sometimes been more accessible to which group of farmers?

B. Wealthier farmers who could afford the necessary seeds, fertilizers, and equipment often benefited more from Green Revolution technologies than poorer subsistence farmers.
4
Mark for Review
Hard

A country adopts Green Revolution technologies and sees national crop yields and food production rise dramatically, reducing overall hunger statistics; however, small subsistence farmers who could not afford the new seeds, fertilizers, and irrigation equipment fall further behind wealthier commercial farmers, and some become landless as larger farms buy up smaller plots. Which statement best captures the complex legacy of the Green Revolution illustrated in this scenario?

B. This scenario illustrates that the Green Revolution can increase overall food production while simultaneously worsening inequality between farmers with and without access to its technologies.

Free Response
A developing country adopts Green Revolution technologies, including high-yield seed varieties, chemical fertilizers, and expanded irrigation systems, resulting in a significant national increase in overall grain production.

AExplain how these Green Revolution technologies likely contributed to the increase in national grain production.

Sample response: High-yield seed varieties, combined with chemical fertilizers and expanded irrigation, allowed farmers to produce significantly more grain per acre of land than traditional farming methods.

BExplain one environmental drawback associated with the widespread adoption of these technologies.

Sample response: Heavy use of chemical fertilizers can cause runoff into nearby waterways, contributing to eutrophication and water pollution.

CExplain why the benefits of the Green Revolution might not be distributed equally among all farmers in this country.

Sample response: Because the necessary seeds, fertilizers, and irrigation equipment can be expensive, wealthier farmers are often better able to afford and benefit from these technologies, while poorer subsistence farmers may be left further behind.
