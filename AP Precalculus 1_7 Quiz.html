If deg(numerator) < deg(denominator) for a rational function, the horizontal asymptote is:

B. When the denominator's degree dominates, the horizontal asymptote is y=0.

If deg(numerator) = deg(denominator), the horizontal asymptote is:

B. Equal degrees give a horizontal asymptote at the ratio of leading coefficients.

Using the graph shown of f(x)=(2x−1)/(x+3), the horizontal asymptote is:

C. Ratio of leading coefficients: 2/1 = 2.

Hard

For g(x)=(3x²+1)/(x−5), the degree of the numerator (2) is exactly one more than the denominator (1). What does this indicate, and why is there NOT a horizontal asymptote?

B. A numerator degree exactly one greater than the denominator produces a slant asymptote, not a horizontal one.

Use the graph of f(x) = (2x − 1)/(x + 3) shown above.

(a) State the horizontal asymptote shown, and confirm it algebraically.
Sample response: y=2 (ratio of leading coefficients 2/1).
(b) Determine the end behavior (horizontal or slant asymptote) of h(x)=(x²−4)/(x+1) algebraically.
Sample response: deg(num)=2, deg(denom)=1: the numerator's degree is exactly one more, so h has a SLANT asymptote (no horizontal asymptote).
(c) Determine the end behavior of k(x)=(5x+2)/(x²−9) algebraically.
Sample response: deg(num)=1 < deg(denom)=2: horizontal asymptote y=0.
