(a) Use the Lagrange Error Bound to bound the error in approximating cos(0.3) using the 2nd-degree Maclaurin polynomial for cos(x). (Use M=1, since all derivatives of cos(x) are bounded by 1.)
Sample response: |R₂(0.3)| ≤ [1/3!](0.3)³ = (1/6)(0.027) = 0.0045.
(b) Use the Lagrange Error Bound to bound the error in approximating e^0.2 using the 2nd-degree Maclaurin polynomial for eˣ. (Use M=e^0.2≈1.221 as a bound for the 3rd derivative on [0,0.2].)
Sample response: |R₂(0.2)| ≤ [1.221/3!](0.2)³ = (1.221/6)(0.008) ≈ 0.00163.
(c) Explain why finding an appropriate value of M often requires knowing whether the relevant derivative is increasing or decreasing on the interval in question.
Sample response: Since M must be an upper bound for the absolute maximum of the derivative over the interval, knowing whether the derivative is increasing or decreasing tells you whether the maximum occurs at the left endpoint, right endpoint, or an interior critical point — without this, you can't confidently identify the true maximum to use as M.
