Free Response FRQ

Refer to the diagram shown above.

(a) Using the diagram shown, identify the group of writers who rejected mainstream conformity.
Sample response: The Beat Generation, shown in the diagram, rejected mainstream conformity.
(b) Explain how television contributed to a new sense of shared national culture in postwar America.
Sample response: As television ownership rapidly expanded in the 1950s, Americans across the country increasingly watched the same news broadcasts, entertainment programs, and advertisements simultaneously, creating shared cultural touchstones and reference points across a geographically vast and diverse nation; this new shared media experience helped nationalize American culture and consumer habits in ways similar to, but even more powerful than, radio had done in the 1920s.
(c) Explain how the Beat Generation's values contrasted with mainstream 1950s suburban culture.
Sample response: While mainstream 1950s culture generally celebrated suburban domesticity, steady employment, and material consumption as markers of success and the American Dream, Beat Generation writers like Jack Kerouac and Allen Ginsberg rejected these values as conformist and spiritually empty, instead celebrating spontaneity, personal freedom, and alternative lifestyles; this cultural critique anticipated many of the countercultural values that would become much more widespread among 1960s youth.
