(a) Use the Direct Comparison Test to determine whether Σ1/(n³+2) converges, comparing to Σ1/n³.
Sample response: Since 1/(n³+2) < 1/n³ for all n≥1, and Σ1/n³ converges (p=3>1), by Direct Comparison Σ1/(n³+2) also converges.
(b) Use the Limit Comparison Test to determine whether Σ(5n+2)/(n³-1) converges, comparing to Σ1/n².
Sample response: lim[(5n+2)/(n³-1)]/(1/n²) = lim(5n³+2n²)/(n³-1) = 5, finite and positive. Since Σ1/n² converges, Σ(5n+2)/(n³-1) also converges.
(c) Explain the key difference between when you'd choose the Direct Comparison Test versus the Limit Comparison Test.
Sample response: The Direct Comparison Test requires establishing an exact term-by-term inequality, which can be difficult to prove algebraically; the Limit Comparison Test avoids this by examining the LIMIT of the ratio between terms, often easier when the series' dominant behavior for large n is clear.
