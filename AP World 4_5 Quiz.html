Joint-stock companies, such as the Dutch and English East India Companies, allowed investors to:

Mercantilism, the dominant economic philosophy guiding European colonial policy during this period, held that:

B. Mercantilism held that a nation's economic strength depended on accumulating wealth through a favorable balance of trade and control over colonial resources.

Mark for Review
Medium

The massive flow of silver mined in Spanish American colonies, much of which eventually reached China through global trade networks, is significant because it:

B. The silver trade helped integrate the Americas into a genuinely global trade network and significantly influenced the Chinese economy's demand for silver.
4
Mark for Review
Hard

Historians point to the massive flow of Spanish American silver into the Chinese economy as evidence that maritime empires during this period helped create a genuinely interconnected global trading system, rather than simply a set of separate regional trade networks. Which statement best explains the significance of this silver trade for understanding global connectivity during this period?

B. The silver trade demonstrates how maritime empires connected the Americas, Europe, and Asia into a single, interdependent global economic system.

Explain ONE way that mercantilist economic policy shaped European colonial strategy during this period.

Sample response: Mercantilism encouraged European states to establish and closely control colonies as sources of raw materials and captive markets, aiming to maximize the wealth flowing back to the home country and maintain a favorable balance of trade.
