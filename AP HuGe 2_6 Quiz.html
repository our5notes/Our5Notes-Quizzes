Topic 2.6: Malthusian Theory and Geography

AP Human Geography · Unit 2

1
Mark for Review
Easy

Thomas Malthus theorized that population grows _____ while food supply grows _____.

B. Malthus argued population grows geometrically (exponentially) while food supply grows only arithmetically (linearly), eventually causing food shortages.
2
Mark for Review
Medium

According to Malthusian theory, what would eventually happen if population growth outpaced food production?

B. Malthus predicted that unchecked population growth exceeding food supply would ultimately be limited by famine, disease, or conflict.
3
Mark for Review
Medium

Critics of Malthus, including neo-Malthusians and technology-focused economists like Ester Boserup, have argued that Malthus's theory failed to fully account for:

B. Critics like Boserup argued that human ingenuity and technology (such as the Green Revolution) allowed food production to increase far beyond what Malthus predicted was possible.
4
Mark for Review
Hard

A modern neo-Malthusian points to declining freshwater availability and topsoil degradation as evidence that Malthus's core warning remains relevant today, even though agricultural technology has so far kept pace with population growth. Which best characterizes this neo-Malthusian argument?

B. Neo-Malthusians argue that even though technology has delayed Malthus's predicted crisis, finite resources could still eventually impose real limits on population growth.
5
Mark for Review
Free Response
A region's agricultural output has doubled over the last 30 years due to new farming technology, even as its population has also doubled during that time, keeping food security roughly stable.

AExplain how this scenario reflects a critique of Malthus's original theory.

Sample response: Malthus predicted food production could not keep pace with geometric population growth, but this scenario shows technology allowing food production to rise alongside population, contradicting his original prediction.

BExplain one specific technological innovation (real or hypothetical) that could allow food production to continue increasing faster than population growth.

Sample response: Genetically modified, drought-resistant crop varieties could allow farmers to maintain or increase yields even as water availability becomes more limited.

CExplain one reason a neo-Malthusian might still be concerned about this region's long-term sustainability despite current food security.

Sample response: A neo-Malthusian might point out that continued reliance on technology to outpace population growth does not address finite underlying resources, such as topsoil or freshwater, which could eventually limit further increases in production.
