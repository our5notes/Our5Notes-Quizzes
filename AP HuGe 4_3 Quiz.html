The connection people, culture, and economic systems have to a specific piece of land, and the behaviors used to control and defend that space, is known as:

B. Territoriality refers to the connection people and institutions have to land, and the behaviors used to control and defend it.
2
Mark for Review
Medium

Halford Mackinder's Heartland Theory argued that control over which region was key to achieving world political power?

B. Mackinder's Heartland Theory argued that control of the vast interior of Eurasia was key to world domination.
3
Mark for Review
Medium

A geographically strategic narrow passageway, such as the Strait of Hormuz, through which a large volume of global trade or military traffic must pass, is known as a:

C. A choke point is a strategic narrow passageway through which a large volume of global trade or military traffic must pass.
4
Mark for Review
Hard

A politically fragmented and unstable region located between two competing major powers becomes the site of repeated proxy conflicts, as both powers attempt to expand their influence there without engaging in direct war with each other. Which geopolitical concept does this region best represent?

B. A shatterbelt is a politically fragmented, unstable region caught between competing major powers, often becoming a site of proxy conflict.

Free Response
A narrow strait connecting two major bodies of water carries a significant share of the world's oil shipments, and a nearby politically unstable region has become a site of repeated proxy conflicts between two competing global powers seeking to expand their regional influence.

AIdentify the geopolitical term that best describes the narrow strait, and explain its strategic significance.

Sample response: The strait is a choke point, since it is a narrow, strategically vital passageway that a large volume of global trade, in this case oil shipments, must pass through.

BIdentify the geopolitical term that best describes the unstable region experiencing proxy conflicts, and explain why it fits this classification.

Sample response: The unstable region is a shatterbelt, since it is politically fragmented and caught between two competing major powers using it as a site for indirect, proxy conflict.

CExplain how Spykman's Rimland Theory might apply to understanding the strategic importance of this coastal, contested region.

Sample response: Spykman's Rimland Theory argued that coastal regions, rather than the Eurasian interior, were the key to global power due to their economic and strategic importance, which would explain why major powers are so invested in controlling this coastal, contested area.
