For g(x)=∫(a to x)f(t)dt, g is increasing wherever:

A. Since g'(x)=f(x), g increases exactly where f is positive.

g(x)=∫(a to x)f(t)dt has a local extremum wherever:

B. Since g'=f, a local extremum of g occurs exactly where f changes sign.

Since g''(x)=f'(x), g is concave up wherever:

B. g''=f', so g is concave up wherever f itself is increasing.

Free Response

Use the graph of f(t) shown above (the same graph from Topic 6.4). Let g(x)=∫(-2 to x)f(t)dt.

(a) On which interval(s) is g increasing? Decreasing?
Sample response: f(t) is negative only on roughly (-2,-1.5), so g is decreasing there, and increasing on the rest of the domain, roughly (-1.5,8).
(b) Does g have any local extrema on (-2,8)? If so, identify and classify them.
Sample response: Yes — g has a local minimum at approximately x=-1.5, where f changes from negative to positive (the only sign change of f on this domain).
(c) On which interval(s) is g concave up? Concave down?
Sample response: f is increasing on roughly (-2,2) (up to the top of the semicircle), so g is concave up there. f is decreasing on roughly (2,8), so g is concave down there.
