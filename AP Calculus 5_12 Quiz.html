To find vertical tangents on an implicit curve, you set:

B. Vertical tangents occur where dy/dx is undefined, i.e., where the denominator is zero.

Medium

For the circle x²+y²=25 shown, dy/dx=-x/y. At which points is the tangent line vertical?

B. Vertical tangents occur where the denominator y=0, giving the points (5,0) and (-5,0) on the circle.

Hard

For x²+xy+y²=3, implicit differentiation gives dy/dx=-(2x+y)/(x+2y). At a point where x+2y=0 (denominator zero) but 2x+y≠0, what does this indicate about the curve there?

B. A zero denominator with a nonzero numerator indicates dy/dx is undefined — a vertical tangent.

The circle x²+y²=25 is shown above.

(a) Find dy/dx implicitly.
Sample response: 2x+2yy'=0 → y' = -x/y.
(b) Find all points on the circle where the tangent line is horizontal.
Sample response: Horizontal tangent: -x/y=0 → x=0 (with y≠0). On the circle, x=0 gives y=±5. Points: (0,5) and (0,-5).
(c) For the curve 4x²+y²=16, find dy/dx implicitly, then find all points where the tangent line is horizontal.
Sample response: 8x+2yy'=0 → y'=-4x/y. Horizontal: -4x/y=0 → x=0. Substituting into the curve: y²=16 → y=±4. Points: (0,4) and (0,-4).
