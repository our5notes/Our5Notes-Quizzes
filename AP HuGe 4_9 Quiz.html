When multiple countries voluntarily give up a degree of individual sovereignty to join a larger international organization, such as the European Union, that makes binding decisions on their behalf, this is known as:

B. Supranationalism occurs when multiple states give up some individual sovereignty to a larger international organization.

A well-organized militant group operating within a country's borders and actively fighting to overthrow the government or seize territorial control poses a challenge to state sovereignty known as a(n):

B. An insurgency is an organized militant movement fighting to overthrow a government or seize territorial control, posing a direct challenge to state sovereignty.

Global institutions and agreements, such as international trade organizations or environmental treaties, can challenge a state's full sovereignty by:

B. International institutions and agreements can require member states to follow binding rules, limiting their fully independent decision-making and challenging traditional sovereignty.

A country joins a regional economic and political union that requires member states to follow shared trade regulations and allow free movement of people across borders, even though this limits the country's ability to independently set its own tariffs or immigration policy. Later, a domestic political movement argues the country should leave the union to "reclaim sovereignty." Which concept does the union itself best represent, and what is the domestic movement's central sovereignty-related argument?

B. The union is an example of supranationalism, and the domestic movement's argument centers on reclaiming sovereignty that membership has limited.

Free Response
A country is a member of a regional supranational organization that sets shared trade and immigration rules for all member states. A growing domestic political movement argues that this membership has undermined the country's ability to independently control its own borders and economic policy, and calls for withdrawal from the organization.

AExplain how membership in a supranational organization can challenge a member state's full sovereignty.

Sample response: By agreeing to follow shared rules set by the supranational organization, a member state gives up some of its independent decision-making authority over policies like trade and immigration, directly limiting its full sovereignty.

BExplain the domestic movement's central argument in favor of withdrawing from the organization.

Sample response: The movement argues that the country should reclaim full independent control over its own borders and economic policy by withdrawing from the organization and its binding shared rules.

CExplain one potential economic or political trade-off the country might face if it chooses to withdraw from the organization.

Sample response: Withdrawing could allow the country to regain independent policy control, but it might also lose beneficial trade access and economic cooperation that came with membership in the organization.
