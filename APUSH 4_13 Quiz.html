Free Response FRQ

Refer to the diagram shown above.

(a) Using the diagram shown, identify the group at the top of the Southern social hierarchy.
Sample response: The planter elite, shown at the top of the diagram, occupied the top of the Southern social hierarchy.
(b) Explain why the planter elite held disproportionate political power in the South, despite being a small percentage of the population.
Sample response: The planter elite's immense wealth, generated by the forced labor of large numbers of enslaved people, gave them outsized influence over Southern politics, economy, and culture; their wealth allowed them to dominate state legislatures and shape laws (including the Three-Fifths Compromise's effect on congressional representation) in ways that protected and expanded the slave system, even though yeomen farmers and poor whites vastly outnumbered them.
(c) Explain why many poor white Southerners, despite owning no enslaved people themselves, still generally supported the institution of slavery.
Sample response: Even poor white Southerners who owned no enslaved people often supported slavery because the racial hierarchy of Southern society gave even the poorest white person a higher social status than any enslaved or free Black person, providing a sense of racial superiority regardless of economic class; many also aspired to someday own enslaved people themselves, or depended economically on a slave-based agricultural system even without directly participating in it.
