For g(x)=x⁴, g''(x)=12x², which is always ≥0. Does g have an inflection point at x=0, even though g''(0)=0?

B. g''(x)=12x² never goes negative, so concavity never actually changes at x=0 — no true inflection point exists there.

Free Response

Let f(x) = x⁴ − 6x² + 4.

(a) Find f''(x).
Sample response: f'(x)=4x³-12x. f''(x)=12x²-12=12(x-1)(x+1).
(b) Determine all intervals of concavity and any inflection points, using a sign chart on f''.
Sample response: Testing intervals: concave up on (-∞,-1) and (1,∞); concave down on (-1,1). Inflection points at x=-1 and x=1.
(c) MODEL: A company's profit has P''(x)=6−0.02x. Find the value of x where profit growth is changing most rapidly from accelerating to decelerating.
Sample response: Set P''(x)=0: 6-0.02x=0 → x=300. This is the inflection point of P, at x=300 units.
