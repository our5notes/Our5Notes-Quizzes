A student notices that sec(x) is always ≥1 or ≤−1, never between −1 and 1. Why does this make sense given sec(x)=1/cos(x)?

Free Response

Use the graph of y=sec(x) shown above.

(a) State the vertical asymptotes of sec(x) visible in the graph, and explain why they occur there.
Sample response: Asymptotes at x=−π/2 and x=π/2 (and repeating), since cos(x)=0 at these values, making sec(x)=1/cos(x) undefined.
(b) Find the exact value of cot(2π/3).
Sample response: cot(2π/3) = cos(2π/3)/sin(2π/3) = (−1/2)/(√3/2) = −1/√3 = −√3/3.
(c) Find the exact value of csc(π/4).
Sample response: csc(π/4) = 1/sin(π/4) = 1/(√2/2) = 2/√2 = √2.
