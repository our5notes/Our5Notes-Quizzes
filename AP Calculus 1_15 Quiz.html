Let p(x) = (2x+1)/(x−1), shown in the graph above.

(a) Evaluate lim(x→∞)p(x) algebraically by dividing every term by the highest power of x in the denominator.
Sample response: Dividing by x: (2+1/x)/(1-1/x) → 2/1 = 2 as x→∞.
(b) Evaluate lim(x→-∞)p(x), and state the horizontal asymptote of p(x).
Sample response: The same limit applies as x→-∞ (a rational function with equal degrees has the same horizontal asymptote in both directions): 2. Horizontal asymptote: y=2.
(c) MODEL: A factory's cost efficiency is modeled by E(x)=(50x)/(x+10), x = units produced. Find lim(x→∞)E(x) and interpret what this means about efficiency at very high production levels.
Sample response: Dividing by x: 50/(1+10/x) → 50/1 = 50. As production increases without bound, cost efficiency approaches (but never exceeds) 50 — a maximum sustainable efficiency level.
