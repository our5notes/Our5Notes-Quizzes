A point has rectangular coordinates (−3,−3). A student computes θ=arctan(−3/−3)=arctan(1)=π/4, but this places the point in Quadrant I, which is incorrect. What adjustment is needed?

B. Since arctan alone only returns QI/QIV angles, points in QII or QIII require adding π to get the correct angle.

(a) Convert the polar point (4,5π/6) to rectangular coordinates.
Sample response: x = 4cos(5π/6) = 4(−√3/2) ≈ −3.46. y = 4sin(5π/6) = 4(1/2) = 2.
(b) Convert the rectangular point (3,−3) to polar coordinates, with 0≤θ<2π.
Sample response: r = √(9+9) = 3√2. Reference angle arctan(3/3)=π/4, in QIV: θ=2π−π/4=7π/4. Polar: (3√2, 7π/4).
(c) Explain why the basic arctan(y/x) formula alone is not sufficient to find θ for every point, and what additional step is needed.
Sample response: The arctan function alone only returns angles in (−π/2,π/2) (Quadrants I and IV), so for points in Quadrants II or III, you must add π to the calculator's result to get the angle actually corresponding to that point's true quadrant.
