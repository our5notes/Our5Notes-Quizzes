Historians note that while twentieth and twenty-first century medical advances significantly reduced mortality from many infectious diseases, increased global interconnectedness through travel and trade also created new opportunities for diseases to spread rapidly across borders, while unequal access to treatment meant that the benefits and burdens of disease were not evenly distributed worldwide. Which statement best reflects this complex relationship between technological advancement, global interconnectedness, and disease?

B. While medical advances reduced mortality from many diseases, global interconnectedness also enabled diseases to spread more rapidly, and unequal access to treatment meant benefits and burdens were unevenly distributed.

Identify ONE way that increased global interconnectedness after 1900 contributed to the spread of disease.

Sample response: Increased global travel allowed diseases to spread more rapidly across international borders than in earlier periods, contributing to the spread of epidemics and pandemics.

Short Answer

Explain ONE way that the HIV/AIDS epidemic illustrated global inequality in access to medical treatment.

Sample response: The HIV/AIDS epidemic disproportionately affected certain regions, particularly parts of Sub-Saharan Africa, where limited access to effective treatment contributed to significantly higher rates of illness and death compared to wealthier regions with greater access to medical care.

Short Answer

Explain ONE way that global health inequality has affected different regions' experiences with disease during this period.

Sample response: Wealthier, industrialized countries have often had significantly greater access to medical advances and treatment than poorer, developing regions, meaning that the burden of infectious disease has fallen disproportionately on populations with less access to healthcare resources.
