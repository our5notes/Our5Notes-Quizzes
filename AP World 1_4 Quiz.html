Both the Mexica (Aztec) and Inca Empires developed extensive administrative systems to govern large, diverse populations across challenging terrain, but they relied on different core economic mechanisms: the Mexica primarily extracted wealth through a tribute system imposed on conquered city-states, while the Inca relied heavily on mandatory rotational labor (mita) rather than monetary tribute. Identify ONE way the Mexica (Aztec) Empire used agricultural innovation to support its large population.

Sample response: The Mexica built chinampas, artificial raised fields constructed on shallow lake beds, which allowed for highly productive farming to support the large population of their capital, Tenochtitlan.

Short Answer

Explain ONE way the Inca Empire's mita labor system helped the state build and maintain its infrastructure.

Sample response: Through the mita system, Inca subjects were required to provide periodic labor to the state, which the government used to construct and maintain roads, bridges, and public buildings throughout the empire.

Mark for Review
Short Answer

Explain ONE way that the Mexica tribute system and the Inca mita system reflected different approaches to integrating conquered or subject populations into each empire.

Sample response: The Mexica tribute system required conquered city-states to regularly pay goods and resources to the empire, while the Inca mita system instead required subject communities to contribute labor directly to state projects, showing two different methods empires used to extract resources from their populations.
