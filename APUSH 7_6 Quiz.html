Free Response FRQ

Refer to the diagrams shown above.

(a) Using the diagram shown, identify the wartime laws that criminalized anti-war speech.
Sample response: The Espionage and Sedition Acts, shown in the diagram, criminalized many forms of anti-war speech.
(b) Explain the connection between World War I and the acceleration of the Great Migration.
Sample response: As World War I cut off the flow of European immigrant labor and pulled many white male workers into military service, Northern factories faced urgent labor shortages, creating new job opportunities that drew hundreds of thousands of Black Southerners northward in search of better wages and living conditions; this wartime demand for labor significantly accelerated a migration pattern that would continue to reshape American cities for decades.
(c) Explain the tension between wartime mobilization and civil liberties represented by the Espionage and Sedition Acts.
Sample response: The Espionage and Sedition Acts reflected the government's desire to maintain unified public support for the war effort and suppress any activity that might undermine military recruitment or morale; however, these laws also criminalized broad categories of political speech and dissent, including criticism of the government or the war itself, raising serious concerns about how far civil liberties could be legitimately restricted during a national wartime emergency.
