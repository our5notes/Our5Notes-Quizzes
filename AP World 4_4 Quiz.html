The Portuguese approach to building their maritime empire in the Indian Ocean during this period focused primarily on:

The Spanish encomienda system, established in the Americas, granted Spanish colonists the right to:

B. The encomienda system granted Spanish colonists the right to demand labor and tribute from Indigenous peoples in exchange for supposedly providing protection and religious instruction.
3
Mark for Review
Medium

Which of the following best describes the Spanish approach to empire-building in the Americas, in contrast to the Portuguese trading-post approach in the Indian Ocean?

B. The Spanish pursued extensive territorial conquest and direct colonial control over large inland regions, particularly after defeating the Aztec and Inca Empires.
4
Mark for Review
Hard

Historians contrast the Portuguese Indian Ocean trading-post empire, which focused on controlling key ports and trade routes without extensive inland conquest, with the Spanish American empire, which pursued large-scale territorial conquest and direct administrative control over conquered Indigenous populations. Which factor best explains this difference in imperial strategy?

B. Existing formidable Indian Ocean trade networks made large-scale conquest less practical for the Portuguese, while the more vulnerable American empires allowed for direct Spanish territorial control.

Identify ONE strategy the Portuguese used to establish their maritime trading empire in the Indian Ocean.
