Mark for Review
Easy

Enlightenment thinkers generally argued that political authority should be based primarily on:

B. Enlightenment thinkers argued that political authority should be based on reason, natural rights, and the consent of the governed.
2
Mark for Review
Medium

John Locke's concept of natural rights, including life, liberty, and property, argued that governments existed primarily to:

B. Locke argued governments existed to protect natural rights, and could be legitimately overthrown if they failed to do so.
3
Mark for Review
Medium

Baron de Montesquieu's proposal that political power should be divided among separate legislative, executive, and judicial branches is known as:

B. Montesquieu proposed separation of powers, dividing political power among separate legislative, executive, and judicial branches.
4
Mark for Review
Hard

Enlightenment ideas spread widely across Europe and the Atlantic world during the eighteenth century through printed pamphlets, newspapers, and social gatherings such as salons, reaching an increasingly literate public beyond just the aristocracy. Which statement best explains the broader historical significance of this pattern of intellectual diffusion?

B. The spread of Enlightenment ideas through print culture helped create a broader public willing to question traditional authority, providing a foundation for later revolutions.
