Free Response

Use the graph of y = f(x) shown above for all parts of this question.

(a) Find lim(x→-2)f(x) and f(-2). Is f continuous at x=-2? Explain.
Sample response: lim(x→-2)f(x) = 2, and f(-2) = -1 (shown by the filled dot). Since the limit does not equal f(-2), f is not continuous at x=-2 — this is a removable discontinuity.
(b) Find lim(x→1⁻)f(x) and lim(x→1⁺)f(x).
Sample response: lim(x→1⁻)f(x) = 5 and lim(x→1⁺)f(x) = 2.
(c) Describe the behavior of f as x→4⁺, and name the type of discontinuity this represents.
Sample response: As x→4⁺, f(x) increases without bound (approaches +∞), shown by the curve rising sharply near the dashed line x=4. This is an infinite discontinuity, corresponding to a vertical asymptote.
