Free Response FRQ

Refer to the diagram shown above.

(a) Using the diagram shown, identify the regions that “new” immigration increasingly came from.
Sample response: Southern and Eastern Europe and Asia, shown in the diagram, were the regions “new” immigration increasingly came from.
(b) Explain the difference between “old” and “new” immigration in this period.
Sample response: “Old” immigration, more common earlier in the 19th century, came primarily from Northern and Western Europe and included many Protestant immigrants; “new” immigration, which grew rapidly after 1880, came increasingly from Southern and Eastern Europe as well as Asia, and included many Catholic, Jewish, and Orthodox Christian immigrants, whose different languages, religions, and customs often made them targets of greater suspicion and discrimination than earlier immigrant groups.
(c) Explain how push and pull factors worked together to drive immigration in this period.
Sample response: Push factors, such as religious persecution, political instability, or extreme poverty in immigrants' home countries, created strong incentives to leave, while pull factors, such as the promise of industrial jobs, greater religious and political freedom, and family or community connections already established in the U.S., made America an especially attractive destination; together, these push and pull factors explain both why immigrants left their homes and specifically why so many chose the United States.
