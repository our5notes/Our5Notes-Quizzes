Free Response FRQ

Refer to the diagram shown above.

(a) Using the diagram shown, identify the 1887 law that broke up communal tribal lands.
Sample response: The Dawes Act, shown in the diagram, broke up communal tribal lands into individual allotments in 1887.
(b) Explain the goal and impact of the Dawes Act on Native American communities.
Sample response: The Dawes Act aimed to force Native American assimilation into white American culture by breaking up communally held tribal land into individual allotments, based on the belief that individual land ownership would encourage Native peoples to adopt farming and other practices associated with white American society; in practice, the policy resulted in the loss of millions of acres of Native land (often sold off as “surplus”) and severely disrupted traditional tribal social and economic structures.
(c) Explain how the popular “frontier myth” differed from the actual historical reality of westward expansion.
Sample response: Popular culture, including dime novels and Wild West shows, often romanticized westward expansion as a heroic story of individual pioneers taming an empty wilderness; this frontier myth largely obscured the actual reality of violent conflict with and displacement of Native nations, the forced assimilation policies of the federal government, and the difficult, often unsuccessful struggles of many ordinary settlers, presenting a simplified and misleading narrative of the West's actual history.
