When sketching f' from a graph of f, wherever f has a horizontal tangent, f' should be drawn:

B. A horizontal tangent on f (slope 0) corresponds to f' touching or crossing the x-axis.

Medium

Using the graph of f'(x)=-(x-2)(x+1) (a downward parabola with zeros at x=-1,2), where would a sketch of f have a local minimum?

A. f' changes from negative to positive at x=-1, indicating a local minimum of f there.

Given a graph of f(x)=(1/3)x³-x, which has a local max near x=-1 and local min near x=1, what should a sketch of f'(x) show at those two x-values?

B. Every local extremum of f corresponds to f' crossing zero at that x-value.

Free Response

The graph of f'(x)=-(x-2)(x+1) is given (a downward parabola with zeros at x=-1 and x=2, positive between them).

(a) On which interval(s) is the (unknown) function f increasing? Decreasing?
Sample response: f' > 0 on (-1,2), so f is increasing there. f' < 0 outside (-1,2), so f is decreasing on (-∞,-1) and (2,∞).
(b) Describe, in words, a possible sketch of f(x) assuming f(-3)=2, including the location and type of any local extrema.
Sample response: Starting at (-3,2), f decreases until x=-1 (local minimum), increases from x=-1 to x=2 (local maximum at x=2), then decreases after x=2.
(c) If instead you were given a graph of f(x) directly, describe the general procedure for sketching f'(x) from it.
Sample response: Identify where f has horizontal tangents (mark f'=0 there); where f is increasing, sketch f' above the x-axis (positive); where f is decreasing, sketch f' below the x-axis (negative).
