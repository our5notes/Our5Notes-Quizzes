A student derives d/dx[tan x] from tan x=sin x/cos x using the Quotient Rule and correctly arrives at [cos²x+sin²x]/cos²x before simplifying. What is the correct final simplified form, and which identity justifies it?

B. Since sin²x+cos²x=1, the numerator simplifies to 1, giving 1/cos²x = sec²x.

Free Response
(a) Derive d/dx[cot x] starting from cot x = cos x/sin x, using the Quotient Rule. Show every step, arriving at -csc²x.
Sample response: d/dx[cos x/sin x] = [-sin(x)·sin(x) - cos(x)·cos(x)]/sin²x = -(sin²x+cos²x)/sin²x = -1/sin²x = -csc²x.
(b) Differentiate f(x) = 2sec(x) − 5cot(x).
Sample response: f'(x) = 2sec(x)tan(x) + 5csc²(x).
(c) Find the equation of the tangent line to g(x)=tan(x) at x=0.
Sample response: g(0)=tan(0)=0. g'(x)=sec²(x), g'(0)=sec²(0)=1. Tangent line: y-0=1(x-0), i.e., y=x.
