1.1

Contextualizing Period 1

Key Points

  • By 1491, the Western Hemisphere held roughly 100 million people across diverse societies (Aztec, Inca, Mississippian, and more).
  • Europe was emerging from feudalism into centralizing monarchies (Spain, Portugal).
  • West Africa had sophisticated kingdoms and extensive trans-Saharan trade networks.
  • These three regions were about to come into sustained contact for the first time.

1.2

Native American Societies Before European Contact

Key Points

  • Pueblo (Southwest): irrigation agriculture, adobe dwellings.
  • Mississippian culture: earthen mounds, city of Cahokia.
  • Iroquois Confederacy (Northeast): multiple nations under shared governance.
  • Aztec & Inca: centralized empires with tribute systems, road networks.
  • Most Native societies viewed land reciprocally, not as private property.

1.3

European Exploration in the Americas

Key Points

  • Motives: wealth (gold, spices), religion (spreading Christianity), national glory.
  • The caravel (fast ship) and astrolabe made long voyages possible.
  • Columbus (1492) reached the Caribbean seeking a westward route to Asia.
  • Cortés conquered the Aztec (1519–21); Pizarro conquered the Inca (1532–33) — aided by Native alliances & disease, not weapons alone.
CaravelConquistadorMercantilism
Trap: Cortés's victory depended heavily on Native alliances (e.g., Tlaxcala) and disease — not superior weaponry alone.
Key voyages, 1492–1533.
1.4

Columbian Exchange, Spanish Exploration, and Conquest

Key Points

  • Americas → Old World: maize, potatoes, tomatoes (fuel global population growth).
  • Old World → Americas: horses, cattle, and devastating disease (smallpox).
  • Native population collapse (up to 90% in some regions) created a labor shortage.
  • This shortage drove the growth of the enslaved African labor trade.
Columbian ExchangeDemographic collapseTriangular trade
Goods, disease, and people moved both ways.

1.5

Labor, Slavery, and Caste in the Spanish Colonial System

Key Points

  • Encomienda: forced Native labor/tribute for "protection." Led to abuse & population decline.
  • Repartimiento replaced it, limiting (not ending) forced Native labor.
  • As both proved insufficient, Spain turned to enslaved African labor.
  • The casta system ranked colonial society by ancestry: peninsulares > criollos > mestizos/mulattoes > Native peoples & enslaved Africans.

1.6

Cultural Interactions Between Europeans, Native Americans, and Africans

Key Points

  • Interactions ranged from conflict to strategic alliance to cooperation and syncretism (blending of cultures/religions).
  • Some Native groups (e.g., Tlaxcala) allied with Spain against rival powers like the Aztec.
  • Africans, Native peoples, and Europeans blended language, religion, food, and music into new hybrid cultures.

1.7

Causation in Period 1

Key Points

  • Causation: identifying and weighing causes/effects of historical developments.
  • Long-term cause: broad, underlying (e.g., European navigation tech).
  • Short-term cause: immediate trigger (e.g., Spain funding Columbus in 1492).
  • Unintended consequences: not deliberately planned (e.g., demographic collapse, the slave trade).
CausationLong-term causeUnintended consequence
Trap: Columbus's intended goal (a trade route to Asia) is very different from the unintended consequences (disease, slavery) — know which is which.

⚠

Commonly Missed on the Exam

  • 1491, not 1492: emphasizes established Native societies existed before contact.
  • Cortés's success required Native alliances + disease, not weaponry alone.
  • Encomienda → Repartimiento: repartimiento limited, but did not eliminate, forced Native labor.
  • Know the casta system order: peninsulares > criollos > mestizos/mulattoes > Native/enslaved.
  • Syncretism = blending, not simple replacement of one culture by another.
  • Intended vs. unintended: Columbus intended trade routes, not the demographic collapse or slave trade that followed.
