Question 4 Hard

Cellulose and starch are both polymers of glucose, yet humans can digest starch but not cellulose. What explains this difference?

AStarch molecules are much smaller than cellulose molecules overall
BStarch and cellulose use different types of glycosidic bonds
CCellulose lacks the oxygen atoms needed for digestive enzymes

Starch uses alpha glycosidic bonds, which human enzymes can break, while cellulose uses beta bonds, which they cannot.
Free Response FRQ
(a) Define monosaccharide and give one example.
Sample response: A monosaccharide is a single sugar unit, the monomer of carbohydrates; glucose is one common example.
(b) Compare the structural difference between starch and glycogen.
Sample response: Starch is a plant storage polysaccharide with relatively little branching, while glycogen, the animal storage polysaccharide, is much more highly branched, allowing for faster mobilization of glucose when needed.
(c) Explain why termites, unlike humans, are able to digest cellulose (wood), connecting your answer to enzyme specificity.
Sample response: Termites host symbiotic microorganisms in their digestive tract that produce enzymes capable of breaking the beta-glycosidic bonds found in cellulose; humans lack these particular enzymes (or the symbiotic organisms that produce them), so we cannot break down cellulose's bonds despite it being made of the same glucose monomers as starch.
