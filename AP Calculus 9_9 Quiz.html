Use the graph of the cardioid r=2+2cos(θ) and circle r=3 shown above.

(a) Find the angles θ where the two curves intersect.
Sample response: Set 3=2+2cosθ → cosθ=1/2 → θ=±π/3.
(b) Set up and evaluate the area of the region inside the cardioid but outside the circle, between θ=-π/3 and π/3.
Sample response: At θ=0, the cardioid (r=4) is outer to the circle (r=3). A = (1/2)∫(-π/3 to π/3)[(2+2cosθ)²-3²]dθ = (1/2)(9√3-2π) = 4.5√3-π ≈ 4.65.
(c) Explain, referencing (b) and the total cardioid area from Topic 9.8, how you could find the area of the cardioid that does NOT overlap with the circle's interior.
Sample response: The total cardioid area (6π) already includes the region overlapping with the circle's interior. To find only the non-overlapping part, you would subtract the area common to both curves (the overlap) from the cardioid's total area, requiring separate identification of the overlapping region using the circle as the boundary where it's smaller.
