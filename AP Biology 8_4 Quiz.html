Topic 8.4 Quiz: Effect of Density on Populations

Figure for Questions Below
Density-dependent factors (disease, competition) intensify in crowded populations; density-independent factors (fire, storms) act regardless of density.
Question 1 Easy

What term describes a limiting factor whose effect increases as population density increases?

AA density-independent factor
BA density-dependent factor
CA carrying capacity factor
DAn exponential growth factor
A density-dependent factor has an effect that increases as population density increases.
Question 2 Medium

Using the diagram shown, which type of factor is a wildfire classified as?

AA density-dependent factor
BA density-independent factor
CBoth types simultaneously
DNeither type of factor
A wildfire is classified as a density-independent factor, since its effect doesn't depend on density.
Question 3 Medium

Why is disease spread considered a density-dependent limiting factor?

AIt spreads faster in more crowded populations
BIt only affects populations below carrying capacity
CIt never affects small, sparse populations at all
DIt spreads at a fixed, constant rate always
Disease spreads faster in crowded populations, where individuals contact each other more frequently.
Question 4 Hard

Using the diagram shown, explain why a wildfire is classified as density-independent, even though it can significantly reduce a population.

AIts impact does not depend on how crowded the population is
BIt only occurs in populations that are already very crowded
CIt affects only individuals living at the population's center
DIt targets specific individuals based on their genetic makeup
A wildfire's impact doesn't depend on population density, affecting sparse and crowded populations similarly.
Free Response FRQ

Refer to the diagrams shown above.

(a) Identify one example shown of a density-dependent limiting factor.
Sample response: Disease or competition, shown in the diagram, is an example of a density-dependent limiting factor.
(b) Explain why the spread of infectious disease is considered density-dependent.
Sample response: In a crowded population, individuals contact each other more frequently, increasing disease transmission rates; in a sparse population, contact is less frequent, slowing spread, so the impact scales with density.
(c) Explain why a natural disaster like a wildfire is considered density-independent.
Sample response: A wildfire's impact does not depend on population density, since it can destroy habitat and kill individuals regardless of how crowded or sparse the population is, affecting the physical environment rather than being driven by interactions between individuals.
