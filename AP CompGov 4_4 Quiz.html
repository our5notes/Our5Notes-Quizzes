The Republic of China, commonly known as Taiwan, is significant in Chinese politics because:

Hard

Historians note that the Chinese government has responded differently to different social cleavages: using economic and policy reforms like rural voting and pension programs to address the urban-rural divide, while relying more heavily on direct repression, censorship, and reeducation programs to address ethnic cleavages involving groups like Tibetans and Uyghurs. Which statement best reflects this pattern?

B. The Chinese government has used differentiated strategies, employing economic and policy reforms for the urban-rural divide while relying more heavily on direct repression for ethnic cleavages.

Short Answer Practice

5
Mark for Review
Short Answer

Explain why the urban-rural cleavage in China involves more than just an economic gap.

Sample response: Beyond the economic gap in growth and income, the urban-rural divide also involves differences in social lifestyle and access to opportunity, which can generate resentment between rural and urban residents that goes beyond disagreements over money alone.
6
Mark for Review
Short Answer

Identify two ethnic minority groups in China and explain one policy the government has used to respond to unrest involving one of these groups.

Sample response: Two examples include Tibetans and Uyghurs; in response to unrest in Tibet, the government has cracked down on political and cultural dissent, including restricting expression connected to Tibetan identity and religion.
7
Mark for Review
Short Answer

Explain why Taiwan (the Republic of China) represents a unique and sensitive issue in Chinese politics.

Sample response: Taiwan operates as a separate, self-governing entity with its own government, but the PRC considers it a part of China rather than an independent country, making Taiwan's status a persistent and highly sensitive issue in both Chinese domestic politics and international relations.
