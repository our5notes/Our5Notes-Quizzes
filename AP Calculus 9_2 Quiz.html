For x(t)=t²−1, y(t)=t³−3t (the same curve from Topic 9.1), with dy/dx=(3t²−3)/(2t):

(a) Find d²y/dx² in terms of t.
Sample response: d/dt[(3t²-3)/(2t)] via Quotient Rule = (3t²+3)/(2t²). Then d²y/dx² = [(3t²+3)/(2t²)]/(2t) = (3t²+3)/(4t³).
(b) Evaluate d²y/dx² at t=1 and determine concavity there.
Sample response: At t=1: (3+3)/4 = 1.5 > 0, so the curve is concave up at t=1.
(c) Explain why d²y/dx² is generally NOT (d²y/dt²)/(d²x/dt²).
Sample response: The correct second derivative requires differentiating dy/dx (itself a function of t) with respect to t, then dividing by dx/dt again — this is not simply the ratio of the raw second derivatives of x and y with respect to t.
