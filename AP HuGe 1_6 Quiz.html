When a geographer studies a pattern, such as income inequality, at the global, national, and local levels, they are applying the concept of:

B. Scales of analysis refers to examining a geographic pattern at different levels, such as global, national, or local, since patterns can look different depending on scale.
2
Mark for Review
Medium

A pattern of voting behavior that appears uniform when studied at the national scale might reveal significant variation when studied at which scale?

B. Zooming in to a smaller, local scale often reveals variation that is masked or averaged out at a larger, national scale.
3
Mark for Review
Medium

Which of the following is the best example of analyzing a geographic issue at multiple scales?

B. Analyzing an issue at multiple scales (neighborhood, national, global) provides a more complete picture than looking at only one scale alone.

A public health official notes that a country's overall vaccination rate appears high (90%) at the national scale, but a closer look at the data reveals that certain rural counties have rates below 50%, driven by local access barriers. Which statement best explains the significance of scale in this example?

B. This example shows how an average national figure can hide significant local-scale variation, illustrating why analyzing multiple scales matters.

Free Response
A news report claims that "unemployment across the country has stabilized" based on the national unemployment rate.

AExplain why examining this claim only at the national scale might be misleading.

Sample response: A stable national average can hide the fact that unemployment might be rising sharply in some regions while falling in others, canceling out in the national figure.

BDescribe what additional information a geographer might gather by examining unemployment at the state or regional scale.

Sample response: Examining the state or regional scale could reveal which specific areas are experiencing economic decline or growth, information the national average alone does not show.

CExplain why examining unemployment at the local/community scale could reveal patterns not visible at the national scale.

Sample response: At the local scale, a geographer could identify specific neighborhoods or industries driving unemployment changes, such as a single factory closing, which would be completely invisible in national-level data.
